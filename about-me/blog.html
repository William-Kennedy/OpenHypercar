September 14th 2025 - This is the first blog entry as before now I haven't had this website. I have documented a few key moments from before this date on the homepage. Today I drove the car properly. Previously I have only tested the motors staticly and driven the car with a tiny amount of throttle in my living room. Today I attempted to drive the car on an astroturf at my Hockey Club. I chose the hockey pitch as it provides a large flat space with no obstacles, perfect for a stupidly fast car with unkown handling characteristics and an inexperienced driver. Sadly there has been a little rain before hand, maing the surface incredibly slippy. I had the throttle limited to just 50%, and with the trigger about 60-80% in it was ripping the glued tyres off. So better glue is required. Still though, a nice problem to have.
In other news I have reset the design from the V4 to make a V5. I felt the highly integrated Aero left room for modularity while only marginally reducing max aerodynamic potential but making it much more accessible, changing designs based on CFD much more easy this way. I have also drawn up some chassis desings I believe will improve stiffness and decrease weight.


October 1st 2025 - As previously mentioned I have been working towards a stiffer more modular chassis. This has been delayed in Onshape wholly screwing me over, deciding to remove my work since I accessed an old design the new one has dependancies from. Then I tried to get migrate to Fusion 360 but gave up on that due to it's annoying hybrid between off/online features. Eventually I exported my Onhsape version to re-upload to a differant document, effectively removing dependancies from older versions and starting fresh.
From there I have designed a sandwich chassis commonly used on production RC cars built with carbon fibre. It should be more stiff than either a single plate design whilst being more modular than a (stiffer) monocoque. Hopefully it will allow me to fix the problem my car has with low torsional strength. I have also tweeked both the servo adapter and rear bulkhead (superconnector) to fix this issue.



October 5th 2025 - The stiff and modular chassis I have dreamed of has finally come to fruition. I have changed the design from a two plate sandwich design to a tri plate design using a bottom plate and two side plates. This allows me to leverage the 3D printerd part's stiffness in bending stress planar/parallel (planerly parallel?) to the print bed. I have also chosen this way in reaction to the lack of space above the batteries connectors, which would mandate an overly thin design which doesn't contribute to bending stresses due to the weight of the car.
Also I have:
Created a split in the bottom plate so that it can be printed on my 210*210mm build plate, which is now a bolted connection instead of a glued one, hopefully allowing for future desig updates with a lower cost.
Created hexagonal meshes in both the bases and the side plates, although the side plates have been specially designed to accept M3 Nuts, which allows them to act as a modular support system for various things needing to be mounted to the chassis, such as floor, sidepods, electronics and other bodywork parts.



October 18th 2025 - Progress on the car has been so rapid that I have failed to post on the blog about it. After the half term exam rush at school has finished I was able to put in the hours required to get this V2 built. Getting the 3D printer back up to full pace in manufacturing the components was, as always, far too time consuming. I do not recommend the Anycubic Kobra Neo.


The new version of the car is indeed much stiffer than the previous version, and weighs only 1003 grams, 2 whole grams lighter than the previous version!
After a few nights testing it on the road with a 40% throttle limit I have found a few flaws which need to be fixed before I push the car further:
1.) Vibrations from the motors and bumps from the road continously work lose the nut which holds the kingpin.
2.) The nuts which secure the base plate scrape along the road as they are too low to the ground.
3.) The prongs from the rear superconnector aren't strong enough: the right one broke.
4.) The front suspension is probably a little too stiff.

So overall the car will require some serious redesigning for me to feel comfortable increasing the power and spending a lot of my time finding a good test spot. What I will likely have to do for V3 is convert the main plates back into a glued format, which shouldn't require printing them out again.. The front superbone will have to get changed as well to adapt for some differant kingpin mechanism, which may involve more than one linkage. Alterations to the prongs should be hopefully be minimal...
